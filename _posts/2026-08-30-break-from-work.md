## What a Career Break Taught Me

A while back I took a proper break from my career — I needed time to rethink stuff, to think about my health, and spend time with my family.  No projects, no obligations, nothing to prove. This post is my attempt to unpack what actually came out of it, what was really valuable and… what you should consider doing even without a career break. Things are laid without any particular order, all of them are equally important.

Let’s start with something which was much more complicated than I expected. A reset to baseline when it comes to stress levels. 

How do you feel when you have absolutely zero obligations? Honestly, I struggled to even imagine it. What do you mean, do nothing and just rest? Read a book without watching the clock? Drop every ounce of pressure and simply surrender to dolce far niente?

Okay, fine, but…
Maybe I'll code something on the side… no.
I'll make a list of things to fix around the house… *no*.
I'll finally sort out all those family matters that have been sitting untouched… *NO*.

It took me a good while to understand that I didn't have to do anything (well, I have a family, so it's not quite that rosy — but generally, I mean a state where nothing is hanging over your head), and that I could just sit in the square with a coffee and watch people go by. Maybe read a book, listen to some music, and be in no hurry to be anywhere.

I'm not good at things like that, so going through this exercise was genuinely hard — but I'd rate it 10/10. It taught me to notice when my stress levels start climbing, and gave me a much better sense of what real, full rest actually means for the body and the mind. 

You can get there without a sabbatical, if you already know that's the goal — but I had to stumble onto it along the way. I didn't even realize how badly I needed it. Now I look at lazy weekday mornings and long breakfasts as a kind of luxury, and I let myself have them from time to time without guilt.

## "Why bother — you'll just go back to the same thing anyway"

Before I left Allegro I had a short chat with the CEO. I met him during his cigarette break (or Żabka break™  — he told me that long breaks like this usually end the same way: you go back to exactly what you were doing before. He said it with the kind of attitude that suggested the whole thing was basically pointless. The world keeps racing forward while you stand still. You fall out of the game, you fall out of the race, and everyone else keeps running. You will come back to the same race, but you will have to chase others, to get back your position…  bla bla bla 🙂

Was my older colleague right about this? Well… maybe 🙂After rethinking things, what I like doing, what I am good at, I was lucky enough I had an idea where to go with my career. Thankfully I was able to find people who decided to give me the chance to work closer to engineering (which was my plan) and both sides seem to be happy with how it goes. This is still a senior leadership role - does it mean I do the same thing? What I’ve learned is that these kinds of roles are complex enough, can have so many flavours (company size, type of company, business domain etc.) that it is to a large extent a different job every time.

Is the race still going on without me? Probably. I just chose to enter a different one. Honestly, I'm done trying to take part in this kind of race. I know, this is a privilege, but in terms of highly skilled professionals, high paced race is not a necessity, it is just a matter of understanding what is enough for you.

## Deepening relationships with family and friends

This one sounds like a no-brainer, but it really isn't as easy as it sounds. Suddenly you're underfoot with your kids all the time. Suddenly you want to talk to them more, and there's actually time to go out and do things together. For me, this was pure gain — no downside at all. I had some debt in this area so a longer job break was a good opportunity to pay that debt. 

And beyond that: reconnecting with people I genuinely missed. A trip to Wrocław to see a friend I hadn't seen in ten years — why not? Planning a mountain trip with old university friends — let's do that. A long vacation in the Baltic sea — I’ve already bought a raincoat! A five-hour walk through a national park with a friend I usually only see once a year? Colonoscopy you've always dreamed about - yes! 😂 These are, without question, wonderful things.

## Making time for the hobby that's been sitting in the closet

I built an electric guitar from scratch and got back into playing. In the same spirit, I started a social media profile about old newspapers — that was really an experiment in social media and marketing. I learned, through doing it, that it's not something I want to keep doing and dreams like leaving all this and starting a youtube channel are… well just dreams. As a new thing in my professional experience I also tried some consulting work — I hope that my advice was helpful.

You can dream up all sorts of projects, but actually having the option, the resources, and the time to pursue them is what makes the difference. Without a sabbatical, it's hard to imagine doing any of it.

## One last interesting observations

The first few weeks came with a strange feeling of doing something socially unacceptable — shouldn't I be at work instead of sleeping until 11am? 🙂
